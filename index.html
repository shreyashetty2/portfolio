PROJECT — 001
Does Prior Training Exposure Bias Risk Predictions? (ASAP Recirculation Analysis)
ACS · Independent Internship Project · Jun – Aug 2026
  • Designed and executed a multi-experiment analysis on a ~1M-row child welfare dataset to test whether a Random Forest risk model scores previously-trained cases differently, using case-grouped (GroupKFold) cross-validation and replicated designs (18 model trainings across 3 seeds and 3 training configurations).
  • Identified a 15-point AUC inflation (0.902 in-sample vs. 0.755 holdout) and validated cross-validation (0.759–0.766) as the trustworthy benchmark, informing how model performance should be reported.
  • Quantified a moderate training-exposure effect (~2–4 AUC points) concentrated among true-positive cases, using paired t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and logistic interaction regression across 17,494 matched cases.
  • Stress-tested the finding by excluding the top 1% most-recurring cases (43 of 4,389); the effect changed by 10% or less, ruling out outlier-driven results.
  • Caught that an initial single-split result gave a misleading "no effect" conclusion — replication across seeds and designs reversed it.
